## 1. What this experiment answers

Two questions, two separate verdicts.

**Design:** Can strangers enjoy **searching dense little places** and **diving from one place into a new place inside it**, with wordless picture clues, for about 15–20 minutes, on a phone or a laptop?

**Method:** Can Claude Fable 5.1 build this in the browser:
- at a quality the founder accepts;
- with little human effort;
- within the cash cap in §12?

World 001 step 1 is a local, NON-COMPETITIVE prototype. There is no prize, no accounts and no public release.

It is **not** a Hunt runtime, not the World Foundry, and not an engine, VR or data-model decision.

**Code generated by Fable is an experiment, not a Hunt architecture commitment.** See v1 §24; it applies unchanged.

## 2. The concept

**Working title:** *Into the Cat's Eye*.

**The promise:** "Look closer — anything can become a new place."

**The goal, as the player sees it:** a small light is missing from the moon. Follow the old sketchbook to find it.

**The shape:** a chain of **six small places**, each hidden inside an object in the place before. The last place, the moon, looks down on the first, and the player dives back home. The street has changed: it is night now. That is the ending.

**Rules the player learns by doing:**
1. The sketchbook shows places. Find the spot where each sketch was drawn.
2. Some things can be opened. Some can be dived into. Look closer.
3. Finding things opens the way on.

---

## 3. The six places (reference design — keep the beats, improve the details)

Each place has:
- **1 sketch spot**;
- **2 hidden objects**, shown in the sketchbook as small outline pictures, no words;
- **1 thing to open or use**, which leads on;
- many ordinary things that respond normally.

That is about 24 finds in total.

| # | Place | Sketch spot | Hidden objects | Open or use | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Harbor street** at dusk: lamps, boats, a fish stall, a curiosity shop; a cat asleep in the shop window | The lighthouse seen between two roofs, from beside the fish stall | A red umbrella; a tiny key on a windowsill | The shop door, opened with the key | Walk-in view of the shop (a dive through the door) |
| 2 | **Curiosity shop**: crowded shelves, clocks, jars, maps | The counter and the big clock, seen from the doorway | A teacup with a star on it; a glass marble | One drawer painted blue, unlike all the others | Dive into the blue drawer |
| 3 | **Inside the drawer**: a tiny world of old letters like hills, a bridge made of keys, the cat asleep on a pillow of socks | The key bridge | A paper boat; a stamp with the moon missing | An envelope with a feather inside; the player uses the feather on the cat | The cat wakes a little |
| 4 | **The cat, up close**: fur like a forest, a collar whose charms look like a little street | The bell charm | A flea wearing a tiny hat; a fish bone | The tickle makes the cat open one eye | Dive into the eye |
| 5 | **The cat's eye**: the iris is a night sky with glowing rings | A pattern of stars | A comet; a tiny reflected lighthouse | The pupil, a round dark door | Dive to the moon |
| 6 | **The moon**: quiet craters, grey dust, an old dark lamp | The harbor far below | A footprint; a lost glove | Find the missing light in a crater and put it in the lamp; the moon glows | Look down, and dive home |
| ✓ | **Ending**: the harbor street at night; lamps lit; the cat now sits outside, looking up at the moon; the last sketchbook page draws itself | — | — | — | End screen with time, finds and hints used |

**Beat rules (MUST):**
- **Place 1 teaches the game.** The first sketch spot must be findable within about 60 seconds.
- **Order.** Each place's "open or use" step is required to move on. Sketches and hidden objects count toward completion but never block the way forward. The player can skip them and return later.
- **Return and change.** The final street must visibly differ from the first visit. The player must be able to notice at least one thing that was there all along but meant nothing before.
- **Moving between places.** The player can move back up the chain at any time ("Back out"). Found items stay found.

## 4. Travel, camera and controls

**No walking in step 1.**
- In each place, the player stands at a **fixed viewpoint**, like looking into a diorama.
- They can turn and tilt the view within limits, and zoom with the lens.
- They can also move between **2–4 preset viewpoints per place** *(guideline)*, with a short smooth glide. Use this only if a place needs it.

**The dive is the only way to travel between places. It is the same on every device.**
- **Targets.** Dive targets are marked physical objects (the door, the drawer, the eye, the pupil, and the moon's edge for the way home).
- **The trigger.**
  - Hold the lens on a target from close range.
  - A thin ring fills over **1 second**.
  - When the ring is full, the dive starts.
  - Letting go before then cancels it.
- **For accessibility,** pressing Enter at the "Look closer" prompt also starts the dive.
- **The dive itself.**
  - It is a continuous camera move with a scale change, **1.5–2.5 seconds** *(guideline)*, **the same length on every device**.
  - No loading screen, no page change.
- **Reduced motion.** A 0.6-second cross-fade instead of the dive. The total travel time stays the same as the animated dive.
- **Where you are.** A depth ribbon at the top: *Street › Shop › Drawer › Cat › Eye › Moon*.
- **Precision.** Each place is its own scene with its own coordinates. Swap scenes during the dive. Never put all six places in one coordinate space.

**Controls (MUST from the graybox on):**

| Action | Phone | Laptop mouse | Keyboard only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Look around | drag | drag | arrow keys |
| Zoom (lens) | pinch, or hold the lens button | scroll wheel, or hold the right button | hold Z |
| Open or use | tap when the verb shows | click | E or Enter |
| Dive | hold the lens on the target | same | hold Z on the target, or Enter at the prompt |
| Back out | Back button | Back button or Esc | X or Backspace |
| Sketchbook | book button | book button | B |
| Hints | ? button | ? button | H |
| Describe surroundings | menu | menu | V |

- Never use Tab for game actions. Tab moves the browser's keyboard focus.

## 5. Searching, clues and fairness (MUST)

**No glowing hotspots and no labels.**
- Nothing glows or shows a label from a distance.
- When the view is centered and close enough on a usable object, the crosshair changes and one verb appears: "Open", "Use", "Look closer".
- Important and ordinary objects use the same affordance.

**Decoys.** At least **4 ordinary usable things for every important one** in every place. Examples:
- a clock that chimes;
- a jar that opens with buttons inside;
- a letter that unfolds, with a doodle but no words needed.

**Density.**
- **Every place is crowded with detail.** The House prototype had 1–5 usable objects per scene and nothing to search. See `context/images/`, and do not repeat that.
- **Target:** at least **150 distinct props per place** *(guideline; instancing is fine)*, plus hand-placed dressing around every find.

**Sketches.**
- A sketch is drawn by the engine from a stored camera pose, with an ink or pencil shader and a paper texture.
- It is generated from the world itself, so it can never go stale.
- It shows at least 2 recognizable features and never depends on colour alone.

**Sketch feedback and lock.**
- The player can "hold up" one sketch as a small card.
- **Getting warm.** Within about 25° *(guideline)* of the drawn direction, from the viewpoint where it was drawn, the card's lines drift toward the real view.
- **The lock.** Within about 10° for 0.8 seconds, the sketch "locks": it overlays the view and fades into it. A soft sound plays.

**Hidden objects.**
- They are shown in the sketchbook as outline pictures.
- They are found by centering on them and zooming until they are clearly visible, then tapping.
- The hit area must allow for a phone finger: at least 44 CSS pixels, however small the object looks.

**Hints.** Every required step and every sketch has 3 levels, shown only when asked:
1. where to look;
2. what to notice;
3. an arrow or a highlight.

**Text.** The only required text is one opening line: *"A small light is missing from the moon. Follow the sketchbook."*

**No riddles.** No typed answers, no multiple-choice answers, no codes.

**Accessibility.**
- Keyboard play of the whole chain.
- Reduced motion.
- No colour-only or sound-only clue.
- UI text that scales to 200%.
- "Describe surroundings" lists visible landmarks and nearby usable things, never hidden ones.
- Every sketch and outline has alt text.

## 6. Trust and anti-cheat rules (founder decision, 2026-09-29)

The founder adopted these for The Hunt. **In step 1 (no prize), apply them as code structure only:**

1. **One judge module.** Every "is this found?" decision goes through one small module (`src/game/judge.ts`). It is written so it could move to a server later. The renderer and the UI never decide a find.
2. **Answer data in its own file.** Keep all answer data (sketch poses, hidden-object positions, the order of steps) in `src/world/answers.json`. It is read only by the judge. Mark it clearly as "to move server-side before any prize hunt".
3. **A local log.** Keep the session log (§8), so later a server can check that a player searched rather than jumped to answers.
4. **No insider shortcuts.** No debug shortcut may ship in the playtest build.

**Not in step 1:** accounts, bot checks, guess limits, ID checks, escrow, published answer fingerprints (hashes). They come with prize events.

## 7. Look and feel

**The house style: "a handmade miniature at dusk".**
- Painted wood, paper, brass, glass, felt.
- Toon or soft painterly shading, gentle ink outlines, fog, soft shadows.
- Textures made with canvas in code; no photographs.

**Each place gets its own colour grade**, in one family:
- Street: blue dusk with warm windows.
- Shop: amber.
- Drawer: paper cream.
- Cat: warm orange and grey.
- Eye: deep blue with gold rings.
- Moon: silver.
- Ending: night blue with lit lamps.

**Style frames first (MUST).** Before the full art pass, make 3 screenshots:
- the street;
- the drawer world;
- the cat's eye.

The founder approves them before you continue.

**Forbidden:**
- real brands, logos, real places or people, franchise characters;
- words on signs (use pictures);
- anything that flashes more than 3 times a second.

**Audio *(SHOULD)*.**
- Procedural WebAudio only: harbor sounds, a clock ticking, a purr, a soft space hum, the dive sound, the lock sound.
- It is never required. Important sounds get captions.

## 8. Technical rules

**Stack.**
- Vite + TypeScript (strict) + Three.js with WebGL2, exact versions pinned, lockfile committed.
- `three-mesh-bvh` is allowed for raycasts if needed. Any other runtime dependency needs a line in `DECISIONS.md`.
- Dev tools: Vitest, Playwright, ESLint.

**Static site only.**
- No backend, no network calls at runtime, no CDN, no analytics, no external fonts.
- `npm run dev`, `npm run build`, `npm run preview`.
- `npm run build:playtest` builds into `dist-playtest/`, with debug removed. `npm run preview:playtest` serves it.

**Data vs code.**
- `src/world/` holds data only (JSON): places, objects with states/transitions/effects (World Package v0.1 words where they fit; 3D fields under `x3d`), sketches, hints, strings, and the answer data from §6.
- Game rules read the data. The renderer never decides game outcomes.

**Save.**
- `localStorage` autosave, plus Restart.
- If storage is blocked, warn and continue in memory.

**Recorders.**
- **The frame recorder,** `?perf=1`, is in every build. It downloads frame times as JSON.
- **The session log,** off by default. The observer turns it on. It downloads JSON with timestamps of:
  - the start;
  - each find;
  - each dive and back-out;
  - each hint level;
  - the finish or quitting.
- **Test mode,** `?test=1`, fixes the clock and pauses ambient motion, so screenshots are stable.
- **Debug mode,** `?debug=1`, in dev builds only: jump to any place, a state inspector, FPS.

**Phone budget (MUST).**
- Each place is a small scene.
- Aim for **≤ 150 draw calls and ≤ 300k triangles per place** on the Low tier *(guideline)*.
- High and Low quality tiers, detected automatically, with a manual override.

**Label.** "Prototype · non-competitive · no prize" on the title screen and in the menu.

**Rights (MUST).**
- Everything is original and code-generated.
- No downloaded images, models, sounds or code.
- Do not copy code from other repositories.
- `README.md` states the AI disclosure: built by Claude Fable 5.1 under the founder's direction, with dates.
- `PROVENANCE.md` lists every dependency and font, with its license.

## 10. Automated checks (MUST)

**At Gate A (graybox):**
- **Unit tests:**
  - every required step and the ending can be reached;
  - no dead ends from any state;
  - save/load and restart work;
  - each sketch locks from its pose, and not from 30° off;
  - each hidden object is found only when properly zoomed.
- **Playwright:**
  - the build boots with zero console errors and zero outside requests;
  - a scripted route (using debug jumps) completes the whole chain;
  - a 390×844 touch-emulation run completes place 1.

**By the end of Phase B:**
- **Same start:** `?test=1` gives the same first screenshot every time.
- **Screenshots:** at least 18 canonical screenshots (3 per place) in `REVIEW/screenshots/`.
- **Keyboard only:** one run completes the whole chain.
- **Frame capture:**
  - in a visible (headed) browser, over every dive and the heaviest view of each place;
  - report the median, p95 and p99 frame time, the draw calls and the triangles;
  - never report JavaScript-only timing as frame time.
- **Leak test:** dive down the whole chain and back 5 times. Three.js memory counts return to baseline, and the heap grows by less than 10%.
- **Clean rebuild:** a fresh clone, then `npm ci && npm run build && npm test`, works.

**A note for the cloud:** if headed browsers are not available in your cloud session, run the frame capture headless. Label the numbers "headless, indicative only". The founder will measure on real devices.

## 14. Pass thresholds (set in advance)

**Design verdict: 5 adults who have never seen the project.** At least 2 of them play on phones.

| ID | Pass |
|---|---|
| E1 | ≥ 4 of 5 look around and zoom without help within 60 s |
| E2 | At 2:00 the observer asks "What are you trying to do right now?"; ≥ 4 of 5 answer in line with the goal |
| E3 | ≥ 4 of 5 lock the first sketch within 2 minutes without hints |
| E4 | ≥ 3 of 5 find at least one later sketch or hidden object by looking around and comparing (observer judges) |
| E5 | ≥ 4 of 5 make their first dive without observer help; ≥ 3 of 5 name a dive or a new place as the moment they remember most |
| E6 | ≥ 3 of 5 notice something in the final street that was there at the start |
| E7 | Median active time 15–30 minutes, and ≥ 3 of 5 reach the ending within 35 minutes using only in-game hints |
| E8 | ≥ 3 of 5 accept a real invitation to play another world |
| E9 | ≤ 1 of 5 feels dizzy or sick; nobody has to stop |
| E10 | The founder answers yes to "Is this the kind of Hunt I want to build more of?" |

**Method verdict.**

| ID | Pass |
|---|---|
| T1 | Desktop (founder's MacBook, Chrome): median frame time ≤ 16.7 ms, p95 ≤ 20 ms, p99 ≤ 33 ms; usable within 3 s |
| T2 | Phone (founder's phone, `?perf=1`, recorded separately): median ≤ 33 ms, p95 ≤ 50 ms over 10 minutes; usable within 8 s; no crash |
| T3 | Initial download ≤ 3 MB gzipped (target); 8 MB is the hard limit |
| T4 | Zero console errors and zero outside requests; the same start every time |
| T5 | A clean-clone build and the tests pass |
| T6 | No dead ends; save, load and restart work |
| T7 | Keyboard-complete; reduced motion works; no colour-only or sound-only clue; text scales to 200% |
| P1 | The production log is written in real time |
| P2 | ≤ 8 hours of the founder's hands-on time before the playtests; the founder writes no code |
| P3 | ≤ 3 fix cycles after Gate A |
| P4 | Cash spent ≤ $200 of the credits |
| P5 | Provenance is complete |
| P6 | The Phase D changes each take ≤ 1 session, with no regressions |
