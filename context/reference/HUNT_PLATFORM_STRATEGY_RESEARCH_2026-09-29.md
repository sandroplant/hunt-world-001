# Let strangers test the zoom first

**For:** Sandro Makharo, founder · **Date:** 29 September 2026 · **Status:** research findings and recommendations for your decision. Nothing here is approved until you decide.

Keep zooming as The Hunt's signature, but make the promise "look closer: anything can become a new place", not "infinite zoom". The other idea, a world that contains a model of itself, is not new, and its two closest games had modest results, so it should be a tool inside the zoom, not the headline. The biggest risk is not which world you choose. It is that no stranger has played any Hunt build, after four prototypes. So World 001 should change. First build a small "zoom chain" of five or six new places inside each other (street, shop, drawer, cat, the cat's eye, the moon), each one dense and searchable with wordless sketch clues, on phones and laptops, and put strangers in front of it. Build the walkable 3D town and "the model is the past" only if that test passes. Keep VR, cash prizes and the Foundry off the active plan, and start the name change and the first sale now, because both take weeks. Honest odds are low today: most new games earn almost nothing, and no part of this idea is protected. A passed stranger test, a Daily Sketch that people come back to, and one paid commission would change the odds more than anything else.

## The short answers

1. **Signature.** Zoom stays the signature. The model idea was proposed because code draws the copy for free and the rule is clear, but *A Fisherman's Tale* (2019) and *Maquette* (2021) already did it, with modest results. Your interim answer holds, with one fix: promise "a new place at every level", not "infinite", and let the last place loop back to the first.
2. **World types.** (a) A dense picture world with zoom pockets has the strongest evidence of fun. (b) A small miniature town you walk and look into is the best long-term home. (c) A chain of nested places is the purest form of your signature. Test (c), built as densely as (a), first.
3. **VR and phones.** Diving into an object is the most comfortable and the fairest way to travel on every device. The research found no study showing that flying is more fun than walking. Keep hunts fair by counting distance in dives of fixed length and releasing clues on a schedule. Browser VR works on all major headsets, but IDC estimates VR headsets fell 42.8% in 2025, so VR is an optional later lens.
4. **Building today.** At $0 you can build code-made 3D places with your Claude plan, or 2D worlds from free museum art and free asset kits. Under $50 a month adds AI images or 3D props with commercial rights. A 4–6-level zoom chain is buildable today in 2D and in 3D, but nobody has published an AI-built 3D chain, so it must be tested.
5. **Foundry.** It is frozen, half-built, and made for a method you are not using. Do not restart it and do not delete it. Keep its ideas as files inside each world (a "World Kit"), and build real tools only after two worlds pass stranger tests.
6. **Daily Sketch.** It is a cheap public test of fun and habit, a lab for clue difficulty, and free marketing through the share card. It is practice for prize hunts only if it uses the same clue rules, and it should never be the prize qualifier. A free daily with no prize is outside lottery law; prizes later need a free-entry skill contest and a lawyer.
7. **Recommendations.** Sixteen changes, ranked. The top five: weekly stranger playtests, the zoom-chain test, fair search design, wordless clues checked by code, and phone-first play.
8. **Changes to the plan.** Yes. Build a search-first zoom chain as World 001 step 1. Build *The Keeper's Model* only as step 2, and only if step 1 passes. Ten more changes follow in Section 8.
9. **Selling.** "Selling" here means getting one NYC company to pay you to build and host a custom hunt world for a team event. Target People Ops leads, HR managers, executive assistants and office managers at hybrid companies of 20–500 people, with a $1,500 founding-client price. Expect about 1–8 replies from 50 leads, and a first paid event 6–12 weeks after the first email.
10. **Names.** "The Hunt" is weak: it describes the activity, Crytek owns HUNT for video games, and Roblox runs "The Hunt" events with about 722 million visits. Test Findarium, Hide and Zoom, Zoomabout, Zoomlore and Magnifind. Seven of the top ten names contain "Zoom", a brand owned by Zoom Video Communications, so get a lawyer's opinion before you choose one of them.
11. **Odds.** Low today. About two-thirds of new Steam games earn under $1,000 and about 8% pass $100,000. All parts of the idea already exist, and every similar hit had professional art, a team, a publisher or years of time. A passed stranger test, a Daily Sketch people return to, and one paid commission would change the odds most.

## 1. Signature: zoom is the promise, the model is a tool

**Why the model idea was proposed.** Fact (project document): your own world notes say "zooming is the core act of travel", with the chain street → shop → drawer → cat → the cat's eye → the moon. The 29 September consolidation then called "a world that contains a model of itself" your signature. Its reasons were practical. In a world built from code, the model is the same code drawn smaller, at another time or in another style. So the copy always matches the town. One layout gives three places (present, past, unfinished). And the rule "the model is the town one hour earlier" is simple to state. The same document admitted two limits: a copy of the town "is not a new place", and the idea "is not unique on its own".

**The model idea already exists, and its closest games did modestly.** Fact: in *A Fisherman's Tale* (2019, VR), the lighthouse "contains a scale model of the lighthouse", and an object placed in the model appears giant-sized in the real room ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Fisherman%27s_Tale)). Players like it (**94.0% positive of 1,538 Steam reviews**), but only 65% of critics recommend it, and one review said it "only scratched the surface" of its idea ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/559330/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Fisherman%27s_Tale)). In *Maquette* (2021), "every action on a table is recreated in the larger area outside". It has **70.2% positive of 674 reviews** and "mixed or average" critic scores. PC Gamer described "trudging back and forth through a barren plain of nothingness to finetune the exact position of a miniature staircase" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquette_(video_game)); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/762840/)). Estimate: on Steam, *Maquette* earned about **$210,000** and *A Fisherman's Tale* about **$280,000** ([games-stats: Maquette](https://games-stats.com/steam/game/maquette/); [games-stats: A Fisherman's Tale](https://games-stats.com/steam/game/a-fishermans-tale/)). VR researchers described travel by "flight into hand-held miniatures" in 1995 ([Pausch et al., SIGGRAPH 1995](https://doi.org/10.1145/218380.218495)).

**Recursion works as a rule and fails as a setting.** Fact: games where "a thing inside itself" is the rule you play with score highest: *Patrick's Parabox* **99.2%**, *Gorogoa* **97.5%**, *Recursed* 96.9%, *Cocoon* 94.8%. Games where it is only scenery or spectacle score lowest in the same scan: *Maquette* **70.2%**, *Recursive Ruin* 79.2%, *Everything* 83.1% ([Parabox](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260520/); [Gorogoa](https://store.steampowered.com/app/557600/); [Recursed](https://store.steampowered.com/app/497780/); [Cocoon](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1497440/); [Recursive Ruin](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1561890/); [Everything](https://store.steampowered.com/app/582270/)).

**Infinite zoom is proven as art, not as a game.** Fact: *The Zoomquilt* "immediately went viral" in 2004, and its makers released a new piece in 2026 ([zoomquilt.org](https://zoomquilt.org/); [organiccity.org](https://organiccity.org/)). István Bányai's wordless picture book *Zoom* (1995) was a New York Times Ten Best book and appeared in **18 languages** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istvan_Banyai)). But the Zoomquilt's only controls are the "up and down keys". *Everything* (2017), a game about moving between scales, "has no clear goal", and Polygon called it "being, rather than doing" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_(video_game))). Researchers call a zoomed view with nothing to steer by **"desert fog"** ([Jul and Furnas, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1145/288392.288578)). Small infinite-zoom find-the-object apps already exist; one has 10,000+ downloads and 39 reviews ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.netbro.zoom.art.puzzle.game.find.objects&hl=en_US)).

**My answer.** Judgment: your interim answer is right. Zoom stays the signature. The model is one cheap tool inside it. Its weakness is that it does not give "somewhere unexpected" at every level. I add three corrections.

1. **Promise "a new place at every level", not "infinite".** "Infinite" cannot be checked for fairness, and every new level needs new art. A finite chain of 4–6 places can still feel endless if the last place leads back to the first: from the moon you see the harbor far below, and you dive back into the street. Your world notes already make looping the main structure (project document), and *Manifold Garden* uses world wrapping "to make the world appear infinite" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_Garden)). The loop is also the natural home for "the world contains itself".
2. **Every level must be a place to search, with a goal.** The zoom is the travel. The game is what you find when you arrive.
3. **The earlier "signature" advice was wrong about novelty.** *A Fisherman's Tale* is closer prior art than the examples that document gave. The model idea has two more weaknesses: players must be taught the rule, and in a shared live hunt a change in the past would change everyone's present at once.

Use the model for four jobs instead: a map you can hold, as in the 1995 "worlds in miniature" research ([Stoakley et al., CHI 1995](https://doi.org/10.1145/223904.223938)); a "same place, two times" search, which *Hidden Through Time 2* already sells as "Reality Shift" ([Rogueside](https://www.rogueside.com/hidden-through-time-2/)); a team format, with one team in the town and one in the model, like the two-player *The Past Within* (**94.6% of 16,875 reviews**) ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1515210/)); and closing the loop.

**Can Fable 5.1 build a chain of 4–6 different nested places?** Fact: nobody has published an AI-built chain of 3D scale dives. The public Fable showcase builds walkable scenery, not gameplay. Fable's own review of its Union Square world scored most art categories **5.5–7.5 out of 10 against targets of 8–9** ([fable51-worlds QA report](https://github.com/PhiloLabs/fable51-worlds/blob/main/union-square-sf/FINAL_QA_REPORT.md)). A June 2026 benchmark of 2,026 browser-3D tasks found **no AI model above 30%** at getting interaction and game-state logic right. It did not test Fable 5.1 ([WorldCoder-Bench, 8 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01869)). Judgment: a rough chain of 4–6 small places is probably buildable, because each place can be its own small scene with its own coordinates. (One coordinate space cannot hold a street and a cat's eye at full precision. This is standard graphics knowledge, not a cited source.) Confidence: medium-low. The cheapest way to know is to build exactly that as World 001 step 1 (Section 8), with tests that check the game's state, not only its pictures.

**What would change this answer.** If strangers dive again and again for fun and name the dive as their best moment, make "infinite zoom" the headline. If strangers grasp "the model is the past" quickly and enjoy comparing the two times, make the model a core loop. If the searching itself is not fun, neither signature matters yet.

## 2. Three world types: search is proven, the town involves, the chain is yours

| | **A. Dense picture world with zoom pockets** | **B. Miniature town you walk and look into** | **C. Chain of nested places** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Setting** | One huge, busy scene: a harbor, a market, a ship cut open | A small harbor town or island village that looks like a hand-made model | A workshop or cabinet of things you can enter: a drawer, a snow globe, a painting, a cat's eye |
| **Spatial structure** | One picture you can zoom deep into; some objects open into close-up scenes | One small walkable area (a 2–3 minute loop), 4–6 skyline landmarks, dozens of "pockets" to zoom into | A tree of small, dense places; one familiar nest (the model) and others that are wholly new |
| **Core loop** | See a sketch → scan → zoom → open things → find | See a sketch → find your bearings → walk → zoom into a pocket → search → return when the place changes | Look closer → dive in → search → carry something out → see the effect |
| **Best evidence (Fact)** | *Hidden Folks* 96.7% of 9,553 reviews, iPad Game of the Year; *Where's Wally* 73M+ books (2007 figure); black-and-white cat-search games 93–99% | *A Short Hike* and *TOEM* 99.1%; *Outer Wilds* places change over time; *Tiny Lands* zoomable 3D dioramas 91.6% | *The Room* series 18M+ units, #1 player rating on Quest 2; *Gorogoa* 97.5%; *Cocoon* 94.8% |
| **Main risk** | Crowded genre; needs lots of art; weak in VR | 3D cost; phone speed; fair hiding is harder when the camera moves | Drifts into one-answer puzzles with no replay; players get lost between levels; each place costs art |
| **What it tests** | Is searching fun? How many hunts can one scene hold? | Do landmarks, walking and return visits add fun? Does it run on phones? | Do "objects become places" delight people without words? |

Sources for the evidence row: [Hidden Folks on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435400/), [Hidden Folks press](https://hiddenfolks.com/press/hidden-folks-1), [Where's Wally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F), [100 hidden cats](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1587560/), [A Short Hike](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1055540/), [TOEM](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1307580/), [Outer Wilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Wilds), [Tiny Lands](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1354910/), [Fireproof Games](https://www.fireproofgames.com/about), [Gorogoa](https://store.steampowered.com/app/557600/), [Cocoon](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1497440/).

**Density is the point.** Fact: *Hidden Folks* has 15 areas, each with "more than a thousand objects", made by a core team of two over about 2.5 years ([Medium, Apr 2017](https://medium.com/@stefanlesser/behind-the-game-hidden-folks-e6198dfa885a)). Your House of the Missing Hours had 1–5 usable objects per scene, each with a visible label (project document). That gap explains more of the House's failure than 2D versus 3D.

**How they rank.** Judgment: each type wins a different question. Type A is the **most fun** on the evidence: searching a crowded scene is the most proven pleasure in this whole scan, and it lives on phones. Type C is the **most appropriate**, because it is The Hunt's own idea. Type B is the **most involving over months**, because people learn its landmarks and come back when it changes; *A Short Hike*'s designer added landmarks on purpose to "pique people's curiosity" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)).

**What to test first.** Judgment: a small Type C chain whose places are as dense as Type A. That tests your signature and the search at once, without walking. Type B becomes the long-term home only after that passes. The genre is alive and competitive: *TOEM 2*, a diorama photo game "all about uncovering hidden details", came out today with a Metacritic score of 85, and *Hidden Folks 2* is due in 2027 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2900640/); [hiddenfolks.com](https://hiddenfolks.com/press/hidden-folks-2)).

**Four rules for any type (from the evidence).**

- **Hide fairly.** Hide things behind objects you can open, or in plain sight. Never behind random clicking. The weak games in this scan failed on obscurity, not theme: Myst's "pixel hunting", *Maquette*'s solutions "so obscure", and *Fez* players who could not tell real puzzles from "false signals" ([The Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witness_(2016_video_game)); [Maquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquette_(video_game)); [Fez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fez_(video_game))).
- **Give every level a landmark and a trail home.** Without cues, people in large virtual worlds were "often disoriented and had extreme difficulty completing the task" ([Darken and Sibert, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1145/238386.238459)). In a zoomable-map study, 80% preferred having an overview ([Hornbæk et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1145/586081.586086)).
- **Reward every find at once.** Curiosity is strong but short, and it can "disappoint when satisfied" ([Loewenstein, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75)). *Gorogoa*'s images animate when you succeed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorogoa)).
- **Hold far more than one hunt uses.** Later *Where's Wally* books added more targets per scene. GeoGuessr runs endless rounds on one world because its pool of places is huge ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGuessr)).

## 3. Dive, don't fly: comfort and fairness across phones and VR

**What the comfort research says.** Fact:

- A review of 88 studies found that teleporting caused significantly less sickness than steering in five papers, and snap turns beat smooth turns in two. Narrowing the view helped in only 40% of the papers that tested it. Faster virtual speed raised sickness (24 m/s was worse than 10 m/s) ([Ang and Quarles, 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2023.1027552/full)).
- Teleport is not comfortable for everyone. In one study, **9 of 25 people got sicker with teleport**, and smooth steering gave more sense of "being there" ([Clifton and Palmisano, 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-019-00407-8)).
- Preference depends on the game. In a 2026 study of 45 people, **64% ranked teleport first in an exploration game**, while 58% preferred walking-style movement in an action game ([Applied Sciences, 25 Aug 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/17/8472)).
- Changing scale is comfortable when the world grows or shrinks around you. In a 2019 study, **12 of 18 people chose "ground-level scaling"**, and sickness scores stayed low ([Abtahi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/I_m_a_Giant___CHI_19-5.pdf)). In 1995, people animated into a hand-held miniature did not feel a change of scale; they "merely express a sense of going to the new location" ([Pausch et al., 1995](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~stage3/publications/95/conferences/siggraph/paper.html)).
- Flying works only with heavy comfort work. *Eagle Flight* used tunnel vision, head steering, the eagle's beak as a fixed reference, and fades on collision ([UploadVR, Dec 2016](https://www.uploadvr.com/how-ubisoft-created-eagle-flight-sickness/)). This research found **no controlled study showing flying is more fun than walking**.

**So which is more fun and comfortable?** Judgment: diving wins on both comfort and fairness, if the dive is short, steady, centered on its target and never rotates. Apple's guidance says to keep the point you move toward "slow and predictable" and in view ([Apple, WWDC 2023](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10078/)). Walking is fine on a desktop, clumsy on a phone (a thumb stick), and should be teleport-by-default in VR. Flying is a later reward, not the way to reach clues. Change your IDEA-001 flight note from "gradual acceleration and braking" to "fixed speed steps with very brief changes": Meta says to keep accelerations "brief and infrequent", and Google found "constant velocity is far more comfortable than acceleration" ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/locomotion-comfort-usability/); [Google, Jun 2017](https://blog.google/products/google-ar-vr/daydream-labs-locomotion-vr/)).

**Keeping one hunt fair for phone and VR players.** Fact: VR is not generally faster. In a 1997 study, VR and desktop users found a letter in about the same time. But when the letter was absent, desktop users took **41% longer**, because they searched the same places again ([Pausch et al., 1997](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr01/cs598b/papers/pausch97.pdf)). A 2025 maze study found no speed difference between a Quest 2 and a monitor ([Frontiers in VR, Feb 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2025.1518735/full)). The travel method matters more: inside VR, one comfort method took 127 seconds against 52 seconds with a joystick ([Scientific Reports, Jul 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-12143-y)). Games handle device gaps by separating players by input (Fortnite), tuning help from win-rate data (Apex cut controller aim assist on PC by 25%), or removing the advantage in competition (the GeoGuessr World Championship has "No Move" rounds) ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/fortnite/fortnite-keyboard-mouse-matchmaking); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/apex-legends/aim-assist-nerf-season-22); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGuessr_World_Championship)).

Judgment: eight fairness rules.

1. **Count distance in dives, not meters.** Every dive takes the same fixed time on every device and cannot be skipped in competition.
2. **Put clue scenes in small places** you search from one spot, so walking speed does not matter.
3. **Keep travel a small share of solve time**, about 10% or less. This is a design target to measure, not evidence.
4. **Release clues on a schedule and score in time windows**, not "first to click". MIT Mystery Hunt releases puzzles in rounds and has run for a fixed time since 2009, so more teams can finish ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mystery_Hunt)).
5. **Give every device the same memory aid:** a map that marks where you have already searched. This cancels VR's 41% memory edge.
6. **Comfort choices never cost time.** A fade-cut dive takes exactly as long as the animated one.
7. **Measure first.** Split leaderboards by device only if a real gap appears.
8. **Flight, later:** opt-in, fixed speeds, and unlocked for an area only after the player explored it by diving (World of Warcraft's "Pathfinder" rule ([BlizzPro, Jun 2015](https://blizzpro.com/2015/06/13/world-of-warcraft-qa-with-ion-hazzikostas-recap/))). Never faster than diving to the same clue.

| Verb | Phone | Desktop | VR (later) | Fairness rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Look and search in a place | Drag, pinch | Mouse, scroll, keys | Head, lean, grab and turn the diorama | Free |
| Dive into an object | Tap and hold | Click or Enter | Point or look, then pinch | Same fixed time everywhere |
| Back out | Back button | Esc or Backspace | Pinch-hold or button | Same fixed time |
| Walk (later) | Optional thumb stick | WASD keys | Teleport and snap turn by default | Speed cap near 1.4 m/s; places kept small |
| Fly (much later) | None, or a guided glide | Optional | Opt-in, fixed speeds, tunnel view | Never faster than diving |

**The honest state of browser VR and headsets.** Fact: technically, browser VR works. Quest Browser, Apple Vision Pro's Safari (since visionOS 2, with look-and-pinch input) and Chrome on Android XR all run WebXR ([UploadVR, Jun 2024](https://www.uploadvr.com/visionos-2-apple-vision-pro-webxr/); [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/web)). Meta released a free web toolkit (IWSDK 1.0) on 24 September 2026 with teleport, snap turn and comfort settings built in, and says over 1 million people use WebXR on Quest each month ([MIXED](https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-immersive-web-sdk-1-0-gaze-and-pinch-webxr/)). Commercially, headsets are shrinking. IDC estimates **VR and mixed-reality headsets fell 42.8% in 2025** while smart glasses grew ([IDC, 11 Dec 2025](https://my.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prUS54033425)). Estimate: Vision Pro shipped about 45,000 units in 2025, against 390,000 in 2024 ([The Register, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/02/apple_vision_pro_meta_quest_sales_drop/)). Meta cut about 10% of Reality Labs in January 2026 and closed three VR studios ([Game Developer, 13 Jan 2026](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/extended-reality/meta-shutters-three-vr-studios-as-part-of-reality-labs-layoffs)). **Rec Room**, a VR-plus-phone social game once valued at $3.5 billion, **shut down on 1 June 2026** ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/)). Quest store revenue was about flat in 2025 (+1%), though over 100 titles made $1 million or more ([Meta, GDC 2026](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/gdc-2026-state-of-vr/)). Only 1.05% of Steam users had a VR headset in February 2026, a seasonal low ([UploadVR, Mar 2026](https://www.uploadvr.com/steamvr-usage-february-2026-steam-hardware-survey/)).

Judgment: phones and desktops first. VR is an optional lens on the same world, Build Later, behind a benchmark. The one input that works on all three headset browsers is "look or point, then pinch or select". That is exactly a dive, so a dive-based design is ready for VR by default.

Fact: step-by-step AI zoom chains drift. **My pick.** Judgment: build the chain in 3D code at $0. It is the most practical way to get six new places in one consistent style without paying for art, and it keeps your 3D and VR path open. Use 2D plates only as the fallback if the 3D places cannot be made dense and readable on a phone.

**Rights you must know.** Fact: the US Copyright Office says material "generated wholly by AI is not copyrightable", and "prompts alone" are not enough. Tool terms cannot add copyright: OpenAI assigns its rights "if any", and Midjourney keeps "a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, sublicensable... irrevocable" license to your images ([OpenAI terms](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/); [Midjourney terms](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32083055291277-Terms-of-Service)). Judgment: what you can own is your human design: clue chains, the world's arrangement, and hand edits. So keep a provenance record of what the AI made and what humans changed. ## 5. Park the Foundry; keep its ideas as a file-based World Kit

**Where it stands.** Fact (project documents): the Foundry was designed to turn human-directed AI images into 2D deep-zoom World Packages. At the 2 July 2026 freeze, its safety parts were in place: a provider registry, spending caps, protection against double charges, an append-only review log and a freeze boundary, with 446 tests passing at that time. Its core did not work. There was no working path to a real image provider, provenance was hard-coded as "fixture", and the package fingerprint (hash) was taken from a draft that was never saved. It has produced **zero real images** and served **zero players**. The July review said the project was "optimizing the cost of producing an unvalidated product". You accepted this: "fun-validation must come before further Foundry work", and Foundry scope is frozen.

**What similar teams did.** Fact: small teams that shipped dense search worlds did not build factories first. *Hidden Folks* was two people. Their first prototype took "three hours", they "threw the entire game away three times", and custom tools came later, to help one illustrator with a repeated task ([Medium, Apr 2017](https://medium.com/@stefanlesser/behind-the-game-hidden-folks-e6198dfa885a)). *Mass Effect: Andromeda* spent years on planet-making technology that "was never fun", then built most of the game in its last 18 months ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-story-behind-mass-effect-andromedas-troubled-five-1795886428)). The engineering "rule of three" says to turn code into a reusable tool only when you use it the third time ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(computer_programming))). Creator platforms are hard even with big money: the median paid Roblox creator earns about **$1,500 a year**, and Rec Room raised $294 million and still shut down ([TechTimes, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326712/20260904/roblox-creator-economy-paid-444m-non-tech-communities-bookings-now-falling.htm); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rec-room-shutting-down-seattles-3-5b-social-gaming-platform-says-it-cant-make-the-business-work/)).

**Is it still needed?** Judgment: its ideas, yes. Its software, not now. Human approval, provenance records, a saved package with a true fingerprint, an effort log and blind playtests are real needs for any published or prize world. But the software governs a method (paid AI images turned into 2D tiles) that your $0 rule excludes and that World 001 does not use. Do not restart it, and do not delete it.

**How and when to develop it.** Judgment:

- **Now:** park "Foundry v1" with its list of known defects. Carry its job as a file-based **World Kit** inside each world. Almost all of it is already in the World 001 spec: a spec template, data-only world files, deterministic generators, QA and playtest scripts, a provenance log, a production log, and a packaging step that saves the exact files next to their fingerprint. Add one field to the production log: **repeated tasks** (task, how many times, minutes each).
- **Next:** after World 002 passes its own stranger test, and was built in half of World 001's founder time or less, copy into a reusable template only what both worlds used unchanged.
- **Later:** build one real tool only when the logs from two validated worlds show one task taking about a quarter or more of your hours, three more worlds are planned, and someone can maintain it. A creator editor comes much later: after a trusted collaborator ships a world with the kit without your help, and after there is an audience and money for moderation, payments and support.
- **Revive the old code** only if you choose paid AI images for 2D worlds as your production method.

Proposed line for your decision register: "World Foundry v1 (AI images to 2D tiles): PARKED with known defects. Successor: file-based World Kit, extracted after World 002. Tools and any editor are gated by evidence. Reopening v1 needs a founder decision."

## 6. The Daily Sketch is a fun test first and practice second

**Why daily puzzles work.** Fact: everyone gets the same short puzzle once a day, which creates a shared moment and a habit. Wordle grew from **90 players on 1 November 2021 to over 2 million weekly players by 9 January 2022** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). Its creator said that with a random word for each player, "it wouldn't have caught on". The emoji share grid came from a player who typed it by hand, and Wardle then built it into the game ([TechCrunch, 12 Jan 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/)). NYT Games had 11.2 billion plays in 2025 ([Forbes, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/erikkain/2026/09/02/nyt-games-bonus-puzzles-wordle-in-1-connections-3x3/)). The best model for you is *Clues by Sam*: one developer, launched in May 2025, **over 50,000 daily players by early 2026**, free with no ads, paid puzzle packs from $1, and a weekly difficulty curve from "Easy" on Monday to "Evil" on Sunday ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_By_Sam)). Counter-case: we only hear about the winners. Many daily clones failed, and nobody publishes how many.

**What it is for, in order of value.** Judgment:

1. **A cheap public test of fun and habit.** Do people come back for free? If not, they will not pay or compete later.
2. **A clue-difficulty lab.** Each day gives one clue's solve rate, solve time, hints used and quitting points.
3. **Free marketing** through the share card.
4. **Community**, and an easy first step into hunts, like Puzzled Pint, a free, prize-free monthly puzzle night with 3,000+ players a month ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzled_Pint)).
5. **Practice for prize hunts**, but only if it uses the exact same sketch clues, dives and hint ladder.
6. **Qualifying for prizes** comes last, and only as a low bar.

**Practice, yes. Qualifier, no.** Fact: free play almost never feeds prize events directly. Strong products put a gated layer in between. GeoGuessr gives free players no competitive modes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGuessr)). Fortnite's cash cups required a ranked division, account level 50+ and two-factor login ([Fortnite rules, Apr 2022](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-cash-cup-hype-cup-chapter-3-season-2-official-rules); [Fortnite, Jun 2022](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-competitive-update-arena-adjustments-competitive-support-discord-and-more)). Public dailies are easy to cheat: 0.2–0.5% of Wordle players solve in one guess, against 0.043% expected by chance, which suggests **4,000–10,000 people a day get the answer outside the game** ([Dilger, arXiv, Sep 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02110)). No product publishes how many free players become competitors. Judgment: the Daily Sketch is real practice when it uses the real clue rules. Prize winners should be picked in a separate, time-limited event with fresh content.

**Design rules and pitfalls.** Judgment, based on the evidence cited:

- One sketch a day, the same for everyone, in one existing world. Aim for a solve of a few minutes (a guess to tune with data).
- Share card: puzzle number, time, hints used and depth reached. Never the spot.
- Keep the answer on a server and reveal it when the day ends.
- Show streaks, offer "freezes", and never shame a broken streak. In experiments, pointing out a broken streak cut persistence from **66.23% to 57.86%** ([INSEAD](https://knowledge.insead.edu/marketing/consumer-streaks-are-motivating-key-keeping-them-alive)).
- **Watch the content treadmill.** A Wordle answer is a word. A Hunt sketch needs a spot, a fairness check and hints. In a code-built world, the game can draw each sketch from the world itself, so a new sketch costs a chosen spot and a check, not new art. Build several weeks of sketches before launch and time each one. If one takes hours, run weekdays only.
- Judge it over weeks. On average, a new daily behavior took **66 days** to become automatic ([UCL, Aug 2009](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)).
- **Call: Build Next.** Start after the stranger test passes, with a one-week private run for 20–50 invited players.

### US and New York legal basics (background, not legal advice)

Fact: an illegal lottery has three parts: a prize, chance, and "consideration" (usually payment). Remove any one of them ([Cohen Silverman Rowan, promolaw.com](https://www.promolaw.com/skill-contests)). In a legal skill contest, "the contestants' talents - not the vagaries of chance - must determine the prize winners" (same source).

| Situation | Risk | New York and Florida rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Free daily, no prize** | No prize and no payment, so lottery and sweepstakes law should not reach it. Privacy, terms-of-service and children's-data rules still apply | None specific |
| **Free-entry skill contest:** fixed prizes paid by you or a sponsor, published scoring, ties broken by skill | Generally outside lottery law if skill, not chance, decides. A random tie-break can change the legal class ([Fasthoff Law, Jul 2026](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)) | NY General Business Law §369-e and Florida §849.094 target promotions where chance picks winners, so they seem not to apply. | **In-game currency that can be swapped for cash or prizes** | Looks like the "dual-currency" model New York banned on 5 December 2025 | Fines of $10,000–$100,000 per violation ([SBC Americas, Dec 2025](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/12/08/new-york-hochul-signs-sweepstakes-ban/)) |

Fact: prize operators also need identity checks, age rules (Fortnite: 13+ with parental permission), excluded countries, US tax forms (1099-MISC) and a payout process ([Fortnite rules](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-cash-cup-hype-cup-chapter-3-season-2-official-rules)). Judgment: build these only when a prize event is actually scheduled. One question for a lawyer then: could "you must have played N dailies" count as consideration?

### How World 001 should change

**The current plan.** Fact (project document): *The Keeper's Model* is a walkable 3D harbor town at dusk. A model in the workshop shows the town one hour earlier. You zoom into the model to enter it, and what you change in the past changes the present. A third, unfinished pencil model sits inside the second. Clues are wordless sketches. It is a careful design, with good rules against labels, riddles and sparse scenes.

**Why change it.** The evidence raises six problems:

1. **It tests three unknowns at once:** 3D walking (the most expensive structure), "the model is the past" (the least proven mechanic), and whether searching is fun at all (the most basic question). If strangers do not enjoy it, you will not know which part failed.
2. **Recursion succeeds as the core rule and fails as mere setting** (Section 1). The Keeper's Model does make it a rule, which is good, but its closest precedents, *A Fisherman's Tale* and *Maquette*, had modest results.
3. **It cannot test your own signature.** The earlier document admitted that only the optional moon secret touches "street → shop → drawer → cat's eye → moon".
4. **It is the hardest version for phones:** the town is drawn twice, and phones are where search players are.
5. **VR, the main reason to prefer 3D walking, is shrinking** (Section 3).
6. **Dense searching has the strongest evidence of fun** (Section 2), so it should be tested first and cleanly.

**The strongest case for keeping it.** You want 3D, and your own verdict is that the flat House felt wrong. Walking exploration is loved: *A Short Hike* and *TOEM* both have 99.1% positive reviews. The spec already has a grey-version gate and a fallback. It also tests whether Fable can build a 3D world cheaply, which is a real question. And your energy matters: a test you do not believe in may be run half-heartedly.

**Does that case survive?** Partly. The House failed for design reasons — sparse scenes, labels on everything, answers chosen from buttons — not because it was flat (project document). The zoom chain below keeps 3D, keeps Fable, keeps the sketch clues and keeps your own images. It tests your signature, which The Keeper's Model cannot. The only thing it postpones is walking, and walking can be added in step 2 once you know the search is fun.

**My recommendation: World 001 in two steps.** Judgment, medium confidence.

**Step 1 — "Into the Cat's Eye" (working title): a search-first zoom chain.**

- **Five or six small, dense, code-built 3D places in one chain**, all in the spec's "handmade miniature at dusk" style: a harbor street → a shop → a drawer in the shop → a cat asleep in the drawer → the cat's eye → the moon. From the moon you see the harbor far below and can dive back to the start. That closes the loop.
- **You stand at a fixed spot in each place**, like a diorama. You can turn and tilt the view a little, use the lens to look closer, and open things. No walking yet. *Moss*, a well-reviewed VR game, gives the player "a spot to be" in every scene ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/polyarcs-moss-hides-clever-vr-game-design-under-its-cuteness/)).
- **Diving is the only travel.** Hold the lens on a marked object, a ring fills, and you dive. The dive takes the same time on every device. A depth ribbon shows where you are ("Street › Shop › Drawer › …").
- **Five or six wordless sketch clues**, drawn by the game from the world itself, with "getting warm" feedback and three hint levels. At least four ordinary objects for every important one. No labels, no typed answers.
- **Touch, mouse and keyboard from the grey version on**: drag to look, pinch to zoom, tap to open.
- **It uses five ideas you already accepted:** scale-locked clues, the one-transition runtime, the depth ribbon, the fog-of-war map, and "guaranteed find in 60 seconds" (project document).
- **Most of the spec carries over unchanged:** no glowing hotspots, no riddles, decoys, hints, provenance rules, the frame recorder, the session log, the state checks, the stop points, the production log and the two verdicts.
- **The test:** five strangers on the grey version, then five to ten on the finished version, with at least two on phones. Set the numbers before testing. Suggested numbers, adapted from the spec's own criteria: at least 4 of 5 find the first sketch within 2 minutes; at least 3 of 5 name a dive or a new place as the moment they remember most; at least 3 of 5 accept a real invitation to play again; no more than 1 of 5 feels dizzy; median phone frame time of 33 ms or less.
- **What it tests:** (a) whether searching is fun; (b) whether "a new place at every level" delights people without words; (c) whether diving reads as travel; (d) whether Fable can build 4–6 different nested places that run on a phone.
- **What it does not test:** walking, "the model is the past", live hunts, or VR comfort.
- **Size:** Judgment: smaller than The Keeper's Model. It has no walking, no collision and no second copy of the town, so strangers can play it sooner. I have no reliable hour estimate.

**Step 2 — only if step 1 passes: add walking and the model.** Make the harbor street walkable and put The Keeper's Model inside it as one nest, where the model is the past. Compare its results with step 1's numbers. Then you learn exactly what walking and the model add.

**If step 1 fails.** If searching is not fun, stop and fix the find loop first. The cheapest next try is a dense 2D panorama (the earlier "option E"); your July fun-test exception (8–12 dense illustrations, 5 clues, 10 strangers) is still open for exactly this (project document). If searching is fun but the dives are not, keep zooming inside places and drop the chain promise. If Fable cannot make the places dense and readable on a phone after one redesign, switch to 2D plates.

**What would change this recommendation.** If strangers in the grey version keep asking to walk and ignore the dives, go straight to The Keeper's Model. If Fable turns out to build a walkable town well but cannot build a chain, reverse the order. If you have evidence I do not have — for example, outside players who already loved walking in an earlier build — share it, and I will re-rank.

**Proposed new wording for item 1 of the World 001 authorization:** "One bounded, local experiment: World 001 step 1, 'Into the Cat's Eye', a search-first chain of 5–6 nested places, built by Claude Fable 5.1 under a revised spec. *The Keeper's Model* is step 2 and needs its own go-ahead after step 1's results."

### The playbook, step by step

1. **Wait for a demo that strangers enjoy.** Judgment: do not email anyone until five strangers have enjoyed the demo. Selling something that is not fun wastes your first contacts.

2. **Prepare in one week, at $0.** A playable demo link that opens in a browser with no login and gives a first "wow" within 2–3 minutes (one dive, one hidden object, one picture clue). A 60–90 second screen recording of a team playing, for people who will not click. A one-page offer with both prices shown; buyers dislike "request a quote", and clear prices make approval easier ([executive assistant guide](https://www.ramenpartysf.com/post/the-2026-executive-assistant-guide-to-planning-team-building-activities-san-francisco)). A simple invoice and deposit method. A tracking sheet: name, title, company, size, source, trigger, dates of emails 1–3, reply, meeting, proposal, won or lost, reason. Do not build booking or checkout software yet.

3. **Find 15–20 new leads a week.** Qualify each on four points: an NYC office; 20–500 staff; signs of hybrid or split teams; signs they run team events. Free NYC sources:
   - **Your own network** from eight years of sales. Ask for introductions first.
   - **The NY Tech Week calendar** (tech-week.com/calendar/nyc). The 2025 edition had 1,020 events and 40,000+ attendees, and its hosts are companies and investors that spend on events ([NYC B2B guide](https://nycb2b.org/blog/ny-tech-week-2026-guide)).
   - **Rivals' testimonials and client logos** on the Watson Adventures, Museum Hack, Let's Roam, Confetti and Handstand pages. These companies already buy team events ([Museum Hack](https://museumhack.com/team-building/); [Watson NYC](https://watsonadventures.com/location/new-york-city/)).
   - **LinkedIn search** by title (People Ops, HR Manager, Executive Assistant, Office Manager, Chief of Staff) plus "New York" plus company size.
   - Write one **trigger line** per lead: a real, recent fact such as a new office, new funding, hiring, or a holiday-party post.

4. **Send email 1 on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday morning.** Under 80 words, one question. Fact: in a 2026 dataset, the average reply rate was 3.43% and the top quarter reached 5.5%; 58% of replies came from the first email; short emails with one question did best ([Instantly, 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)). Example:
   > Subject: holiday game for {Company}'s hybrid team
   >
   > Hi {First name}, saw {trigger}. I build browser treasure-hunt worlds. Teams zoom into a picture world, search, open objects and solve picture clues together. No app, no words to translate. It works on phones in the room and laptops at home. Here's a 2-minute playable demo: {link}. Would a custom {Company} world for your {month} event be worth a 15-minute look? — Sandro

5. **Add a soft LinkedIn touch.** View their profile one or two days before email 1. Send a connection request with no pitch around day 3. Like or comment on a real post if there is one. Never pitch in LinkedIn messages.

6. **Follow-up 1 on day 7, in the same thread, written like a reply.** Fact: a second step that "feels like a reply" did about 30% better ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)).
   > Hi {First name}, quick follow-up on my note below. Worth a look? I can put your logo and 2–3 team in-jokes into the demo so you see it before deciding. — Sandro

7. **Follow-up 2 on day 14, the last one. Then stop.**
   > Last note from me. Two options if useful: (1) a 60-minute hosted game for up to 40 people, or (2) a custom {Company} world for your January kickoff. If neither fits, reply "not now" and I'll stop here.

   Trade-off: three emails is fewer than the 4–7 touches that did best in the 2026 data, but most replies come from the first email, and the LinkedIn touch helps.

8. **Send safely.** Send 10–20 emails a day from a normal work inbox. Keep bounced emails under 2%. Set up your domain's SPF, DKIM and DMARC records ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)).

9. **Run a 15–20 minute call.** Ask: event date, headcount, how many in the room and how many remote, devices, who approves spending, what "a great event" means, and what they did last time and what it cost. Let them play the demo live. Close with the two options and a date. Send the one-page proposal the same day.

10. **Price the pilot.**
    - **Option A — a hosted game in your demo world:** $30 per person, $600 minimum (up to 20 people), up to about 60 players, 60–75 minutes.
    - **Option B — a founding-client custom world (the real target):** list price about $3,500; the first three clients pay $1,500 flat for up to 50 players. It includes their branding, up to 5 custom objects or clues, one hosted session and a replay link for 30 days. Terms: 50% deposit to book, 50% after the event. In return: a written testimonial, permission to show their logo, a short case-study interview and one referral if they are happy.
    - Keep every price under $5,000, so one manager can usually approve it alone. This is a rule of thumb from event sponsorship, not a team-building study ([i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)). Do not work for free; a paid pilot is the test.

11. **Deliver.** Before (1–3 weeks): a 20-minute kickoff; collect their logo, colors, 5–10 photos, 3–5 in-jokes, headcount and devices; build; send a preview link; test on an iPhone, an Android phone and a laptop; send the calendar invite with the join link. The session (60–75 minutes): a 5-minute welcome, teams of 3–5, about 45 minutes of play, then a 10-minute reveal with the leaderboard, the fastest team, the best find and the hardest clue explained. After (within 24 hours): a recap email with the leaderboard and screenshots, a 3-question survey ("Was this a good use of your time? 1–5"), a request for a testimonial or Google review, and the balance invoice. Within two weeks: a one-page case study for their approval.

12. **Grow from the first client.** Ask for referrals at the recap, when goodwill is highest. Offer the next season (January kickoff, spring onboarding, summer, holidays) with a small returning-client discount; Confetti offers $75 off ([Confetti](https://www.withconfetti.com/pages/post-event)). Open a free Google Business Profile after the first event.

That gives about **1 reply** at a strict average (0.45% of emails get a real human reply ([Belkins, 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates))), about **5** at Instantly's average, and about **8** at its top quarter. Estimate: many replies will be "no" or "not now", so expect 0–3 real conversations and roughly a 20–40% chance of a first sale from the first 50 leads. Plan for 150–250 leads over 6–8 weeks. Estimate: first deposit about 3–6 weeks after the first email; first paid event about 6–12 weeks after, because events are booked 2–8 weeks ahead and custom builds take 1–3 weeks ([Handstand](https://handstandwith.us/team-building/the-experience); [Next Clue](https://next-clue.com/)).

14. **Mind the calendar.** December events are booked 4–8 weeks ahead, so holiday emails must land by late October or mid-November. Judgment: your demo will not be stranger-tested by then. Aim at January kickoffs and onboarding, with emails from late November.

15. **Run two cheap side tracks (a few hours a week).** Donate one custom world to an NYC nonprofit auction; Let's Roam donated an event "valued at $525" to one ([BetterWorld](https://weneutral.betterworld.org/auctions/we-neutrals-2023-earth-month-sil/items/lets-roam-2-family-scavenger-hun-3)). Skilled solvers will test clue fairness fast.

**What would change this playbook.** If 150+ targeted emails bring fewer than two positive replies, move effort to nonprofits and event hosts. If an author, publisher or museum replies with real interest, follow it: the story fit is better.

5. **Player tests with 20–30 people, including non-native speakers.** Next-day recall ("which names do you remember?"), say-and-spell (say the name once; ask them to type it), fit ("which fits a game where you zoom into worlds to find hidden things?"), and find-it (search it in an app store). Set the pass rule first, for example: at least 70% spell it right after hearing it once. These methods are practitioner practice, not cited research.
6. **A lawyer's full search** for your final one or two names before any logo, merchandise, contract or public launch. File in classes 9 and 41 at least. The US base fee is $350 per class ([USPTO fees](https://www.uspto.gov/learning-and-resources/fees-and-payment/uspto-fee-schedule)).

## 11. Honest odds are low now; three milestones would move them most

**Uniqueness and novelty.** Fact: every part of the idea already exists. Infinite zoom: films in 1968, Bányai's book in 1995, the Zoomquilt in 2004 ([zoomquilt.org](https://zoomquilt.org/)). A world with an enterable model of itself: *A Fisherman's Tale* (2019), *Maquette* (2021), and VR research from 1995. The same place at two times, where the past changes the present: *Day of the Tentacle*, *Dishonored 2* (2016) and *The Past Within* (2022) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Tentacle); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishonored_2)). Zoomable hidden-object search in two states: *Hidden Through Time 2* and *Tiny Lands*. Infinite-zoom find-the-object apps are on Google Play and the App Store ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.netbro.zoom.art.puzzle.game.find.objects&hl=en_US); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/infinite-zoom-art-game/id6477912343)). Wordless picture clues: *Gorogoa*, *Tunic* and GeoGuessr. Judgment: the scan did not find the full combination: a dense, fair search world where zooming into objects is how you travel to new places, used for live shared hunts with picture clues whose fairness is checked by code. Confidence that the combination is new: low to moderate, because general web and app-store search was limited. It is execution novelty: real, but easy to copy.

**AI will make pretty worlds cheap.** Fact: world-generating AI companies raised huge sums in 2026 (World Labs $1 billion; Decart $300 million; Odyssey $310 million), and Google released Project Genie to consumers, although its worlds allow only 60 seconds of exploration ([World Labs](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/funding-2026); [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjt9ncukgl); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/17/world-model-maker-odyssey-nabs-1-45b-valuation-backed-by-amazon-and-other-big-names/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Genie_(website))). Judgment: "a beautiful place you can move through" will stop being special within a few years. What stays hard to copy is a fair, designed hunt with answers that can be checked, plus taste, a ritual, a community and trust.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is no longer "which world should The Hunt build?" It is "which cheap test answers the most important questions without mixing them up?" The zoom chain does that. It also joins your two signature ideas without a fight: a finite, checkable chain of new places feels endless when its last place contains its first. So "infinite" becomes a loop you can audit, not a promise you cannot keep. The deeper lesson is about defense. Zoom and recursion are decades old, and AI companies are spending billions to make pretty explorable worlds cheap. What those tools do worst today — holding a fair, fixed answer that code can check — is exactly what wordless, code-checked sketch clues do best. That is where The Hunt can be hard to copy.

The second lesson is about rhythm. The hunts people love for years, from MIT Mystery Hunt to Puzzled Pint, run on a steady schedule and a community, not on the biggest prize. The hunts that died were broken by cash, cheating, insiders or organizers who were a single point of failure. A digital, prize-free daily ritual on a code-built world avoids every one of those failures. For the next months, measure progress in strangers who played, players who came back, and invoices paid, not in documents written.

## Words used in this report

- **Base rate:** how often something happens in a large group, for example "8% of new Steam games pass $100,000". It is the starting point before you look at your own case.
- **Build Now / Next / Later:** do it in the current step / only after the current test passes / only after bigger conditions are met.
- **CC0 / public domain:** free to use for anything, even for money, without asking.
- **Cold email:** an email to someone who does not know you yet.
- **Consideration** (legal): what a player gives to enter, usually money. Prize + chance + consideration = an illegal lottery.
- **Conversion:** the share of possible customers who become paying customers.
- **Descriptive mark:** a brand name that only describes the product, like "The Hunt" for a hunt game. The law protects it weakly.
- **Desert fog:** a zoomed view with nothing in it to help you decide where to go.
- **Diorama:** a small, detailed model scene seen from outside, like a museum display.
- **Dive:** the move from one place into a smaller place inside it.
- **Grey version (graybox):** a first build with plain shapes and no final art, used to test whether the game is fun.
- **Hash (fingerprint):** a short code calculated from a file. If the file changes, the code changes.
- **Lead** (say "leed"): a possible customer.
- **Nest / nested place:** a place inside another place, entered by zooming.
- **Pilot / founding client:** a first, small, paid trial with an early customer, usually at a discount in return for feedback and a reference.
- **Provenance:** a record of where each piece came from, and who made or changed it.
- **Signature:** the one thing people remember and say about the game.
- **Sweepstakes:** a promotion where chance picks the winner.
- **WebXR:** the web standard that lets a website run inside a VR headset.
