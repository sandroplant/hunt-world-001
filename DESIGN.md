# DESIGN.md — Into the Cat's Eye (World 001, step 1)

**No spoilers here.** This file describes the places, the rules and the look. It does not say where any sketch spot or hidden object is. Those are in `SOLUTIONS.md`. The hidden objects are named here because the player sees their outlines in the sketchbook anyway.

**Status:** Redesigned once after Gate A, 2026-09-29 (`DECISIONS.md` D-008). Waiting for the founder at the second STOP A.
**Governing spec:** `SPEC_WORLD_001_STEP_1_INTO_THE_CATS_EYE.md`. Where this file and the spec differ, the spec wins, unless the founder approves a change listed in `PLAN.md` §1.

---

## 1. The promise and the goal

**The promise:** "Look closer — anything can become a new place."

**The goal, as the player sees it:** a small light is missing from the moon. Follow the old sketchbook to find it.

**The only required text:** *"A small light is missing from the moon. Follow the sketchbook."*

**Three rules the player learns by doing:**
1. The sketchbook shows places. Find the spot where each sketch was drawn.
2. Some things can be opened. Some can be dived into. Look closer.
3. Finding things opens the way on.

**Length:** about 15–20 minutes for a player who finds most things. Nobody is timed against a clock inside the game.

---

## 2. The shape of the game

A chain of six small places. Each place is hidden inside an object in the place before it. The last place, the moon, looks down on the first, and the player dives back home. The street has changed: it is night. That is the ending.

```
Street › Shop › Drawer › Cat › Eye › Moon › (dive home) › Street at night
```

Each place holds:
- **1 sketch spot** — a view the sketchbook shows. Find where it was drawn.
- **2 hidden objects** — shown in the sketchbook as small outline pictures, no words.
- **1 thing to open or use** — the required step. It leads on.
- **many ordinary things** that respond normally. They are the crowd the important things hide in.

That is 24 finds. Only the six required steps (and the dives) are needed to finish. Sketches and hidden objects count toward completion but never block the way. A player can skip them and come back.

**What "find the spot" means with no walking.** The player stands on one of 3–5 stand spots in each place, like looking into a diorama from a few chosen places. Each spot allows a full turn and a limited tilt. A sketch spot is therefore: *which stand spot*, *which direction*, and *how far zoomed in*. The lens matters. Several sketches are drawn zoomed in on a detail, so turning alone is not enough.

---

## 3. The six places

The details below are the reference design. They keep the spec's beats. Each place has its own colour grade, its own scale, and its own landmark that the player can always find again.

### Place 1 — Harbor street, at dusk
**Colour grade:** blue dusk with warm windows.
**What is here:** a short cobbled street running down to the harbor. On the left, a row of tall narrow houses; one of them is the curiosity shop, its window crowded with things and a cat asleep inside. On the right, a fish stall with its awning half down, crates, nets, a bicycle. Beyond, the quay, two small boats, mooring posts, gulls. Far off, on the headland, a lighthouse. Lamps line the street, not yet lit. Above the roofs, a dull grey moon hangs low. It looks wrong, but nothing says so.
**Landmark:** the lighthouse.
**Viewpoints:** two. The middle of the street beside the fish stall. The step of the curiosity shop.
**Hidden objects (outlines in the sketchbook):** a red umbrella; a ship in a bottle.
**Leads on:** through the shop door into the shop.
**Job:** this place teaches the game. Turning, zooming, opening things, finding the first sketch, and the first dive all happen here. The first sketch must be findable within about 60 seconds.

Layout, seen from above (not to scale; no spots marked):

```
            headland ....... lighthouse
    ~~~~~~~~ harbor basin ~~~~~~~~ boats ~~~~
    ================ quay ====================
   |house|house| SHOP |house|      | fish  |
   | door| door|window|     |      | stall |bicycle
   |     |     |  cat |     |      | crates|
         cobbled street  ·  lamps  ·  gulls
                     [player starts here]
```

### Place 2 — The curiosity shop
**Colour grade:** amber.
**What is here:** a shop so full that the walls are not visible. Shelves to the ceiling: clocks, jars, maps, globes, cups, bottles, a birdcage, a stuffed fish, a music box, brass instruments. A long counter with scales and a bell. Behind the counter, a tall clock. On one wall, a chest of many small drawers, all the same brown, except one painted blue.
**Landmark:** the tall clock.
**Viewpoints:** two. The doorway (where the dive lands). Beside the chest of drawers.
**Hidden objects:** a teacup with a star on it; a glass marble.
**Leads on:** into the blue drawer.

### Place 3 — Inside the drawer
**Colour grade:** paper cream.
**What is here:** the drawer is a tiny world. Old letters lie in folds like hills. A spilled inkwell has become a dark pool. A bridge made of keys crosses it. Stamps stand in a stack like a tower. Thimbles, spools, buttons, a matchbox, a ribbon. At the far end, the cat is asleep on a pillow of socks. It is the same cat from the shop window, and it is far too big to be here. That is the point.
**Landmark:** the sleeping cat.
**Viewpoints:** two. The drawer's front lip. The top of the letter hill, near the key bridge.
**Hidden objects:** a paper boat; a stamp with the moon missing from its picture.
**Leads on:** into the cat.

### Place 4 — The cat, up close
**Colour grade:** warm orange and grey.
**What is here:** fur like a forest. Each hair is a tall stalk. Between them: a burr, a moth, a knot, a drop of milk. The collar runs past like a road, and its charms stand along it like the fronts of little houses: a fish, a house, a bell, a heart, a tag with a paw print. Above everything, the ear. The cat's closed eye is a soft hill to one side. The cat breathes; the whole place rises and falls slowly.
**Landmark:** the bell charm on the collar.
**Viewpoints:** two. On the cat's cheek, below the ear. At the collar, among the charms.
**Hidden objects:** a flea wearing a tiny hat; a fish bone.
**Leads on:** into the eye.

### Place 5 — The cat's eye
**Colour grade:** deep blue with gold rings.
**What is here:** the iris is a night sky. Gold rings glow in it. Stars are scattered between the rings. A bright wet highlight reflects things that should not be here: a lamp, a window, and something far off. In the middle, the pupil: a dark slit.
**Landmark:** the pupil.
**Viewpoints:** two. The iris edge. A gold ring near the pupil.
**Hidden objects:** a comet; a tiny reflected lighthouse.
**Leads on:** through the pupil to the moon.

### Place 6 — The moon
**Colour grade:** silver.
**What is here:** quiet craters and grey dust. A low rise with an old lamp on it, dark. Rocks, a small meteorite, cracks, a flag with a picture on it, a telescope on a stand. From the rise, the moon's edge, and far below it, the harbor: tiny, with the lighthouse blinking.
**Landmark:** the old lamp.
**Viewpoints:** two. The crater field. The rise, beside the lamp and the moon's edge.
**Hidden objects:** a footprint; a lost glove.
**Leads on:** the missing light goes into the lamp. The moon glows. Look down, and dive home.

### Ending — The harbor street at night
The player lands back in place 1. It is night. The lamps are lit. The windows are warm. The moon between the roofs now glows. The cat is no longer in the shop window; it sits outside on the step, looking up at the moon. The last page of the sketchbook draws itself. Then the end screen shows time, finds, and hints used, with **Keep looking** and **Restart**.

**At least one thing was there all along and meant nothing before.** Several are planned, so that different players can notice different ones: the dull moon above the roofs from the very first view; the cat in the shop window; the shop's painted sign; the unlit lamps.

## 4. Moving and travelling

**Moving inside a place: walking.** Tap or click anywhere on the ground and the player walks there at a fixed speed for the place, facing the same way. On a keyboard, W A S D walk. Walls, furniture and the edges of a place block; the player slides along them or stops. Hills and rises can be walked up. There are no marks on the ground and nothing to find before you can move.

**Looking.** A full 360° turn everywhere. Up and down are limited per place. The world follows the finger or mouse: drag right to look left, drag down to look up. Arrow keys turn; zoomed in, they turn more slowly.

**Seeing what can be entered.** The next place is visible inside its opening before the dive: the lit shop and the sleeping cat through the shop's open door and its window; the drawer world inside the open blue drawer; the cat's fur in the cat's neck; the iris in the open eye; the moon inside the round pupil; the night street below the moon's edge. These openings are live pictures of the real next place, not paintings. An opening you can go into glows softly at its rim. Plain scenery never reacts to the crosshair; only things you can act on do. The dull moon in the street sky is scenery.

**The first place teaches.** The shop door stands open from the start. Nothing is required in the street: look, walk to the door, hold the lens on it. The first dive is possible within a minute. The street keeps only six ordinary things to use; the sketch and the two hidden things are there for players who look. The first lock comes in the shop.

**The dive is the only way between places, and it is the same on every device.**
- Dive targets are physical openings: the open shop door, the open blue drawer, the cat's neck, the open eye, the round pupil, and the moon's edge.
- Hold the lens on an opening from close range. A thin ring fills over 1 second. When it is full, the dive starts. Let go before then and it cancels. Tapping, clicking, E or Enter on a centered opening also starts it.
- The dive is one continuous forward move: the camera flies into the opening until the opening fills the screen, and at that instant the real next place takes over from the same camera position, so there is no cut. It continues forward into the place. About 2 seconds, the same length everywhere. No loading screen. No page change.
- **Reduced motion:** a 0.6-second cross-fade instead. The total travel time stays the same.
- **Back out** is always available. It runs the same path in reverse and returns the player to where they dived from, facing the opening. Found things stay found.

**Locked things show it without words.** A locked drawer has a small dark keyhole mark and rattles when used. The dark lamp on the moon has an empty socket and rattles too.

**If nothing happens for 45 seconds,** the most useful next thing glints once. No text.

**The trail** at the top shows the places already reached by name, and the places ahead as dots. It is not a control and does not look like one. On phones it sits below the buttons.

| Zoom (lens) | pinch, or hold the lens button | scroll wheel, or hold the right button | hold Z |

| Describe surroundings | menu | menu | V |

Tab is never a game key. The start screen shows three pictures with a few words each: drag to look, tap the ground to walk, hold the lens on a glowing opening. The same three pictures sit in the view until each has been done once.

## 6. Searching, clues and fairness

**No glowing hotspots and no labels.** Nothing glows or shows a label from a distance. When the view is centered and close enough on a usable thing, the crosshair changes and one verb appears: *Open*, *Use*, *Take*, *Look closer*. Important and ordinary things use exactly the same affordance.

**Decoys.** Every place has at least 4 ordinary usable things for every important one. Ordinary things do ordinary things: a clock chimes, a jar opens and has buttons in it, a letter unfolds and shows a doodle, a bell rings, a shutter closes. Clicking everything is not a winning strategy, because there is a lot of everything.

**Density.** Every place is crowded with detail: at least 150 distinct props per place, with hand-placed dressing around every find. The House prototype (see `context/images/`) had 1–5 usable objects per scene, each with a label, and nothing to search. This build does the opposite.

**The sketchbook.**
- It opens on the first page: the opening line and the first sketch.
- When the player arrives in a new place, that place's page appears: its sketch, and the outlines of its two hidden objects.
- The last page shows the goal as a picture: the dark moon and its empty lamp. At the end, this page draws itself again.
- The player can "hold up" one sketch as a small card while looking around.
- Sketches are drawn by the engine from a stored camera pose, with an ink shader and a paper texture. They come from the world itself, so they can never go stale. Each shows at least two recognizable features and never depends on colour alone.

**Getting warm and the lock.** From the viewpoint where a sketch was drawn, within about 25° of its direction, the held-up card's lines drift toward the real view. Within about 10° and the right zoom band, for 0.8 seconds, the sketch locks: it overlays the view and fades into it, with a soft sound. Nothing is typed or confirmed.

**Hidden objects.** Center on the object and zoom until it is clearly visible, then tap. The hit area is at least 44 CSS pixels, however small the object looks. A tap that is not zoomed enough does nothing special; the object is just part of the scene.

**Hints.** Every required step, every sketch and every hidden object has three levels, shown only when asked:
1. where to look;
2. what to notice;
3. an arrow or a highlight.

**No riddles.** No typed answers, no multiple-choice, no codes.

**Every find is rewarded at once:** a sound, a small motion in the world, a mark in the sketchbook.

## 7. Look and feel

**House style: "a handmade miniature at dusk".** Painted wood, paper, brass, glass, felt. Toon or soft painterly shading, gentle ink outlines, fog, soft shadows. All textures are made with canvas in code. No photographs. Simple shapes are fine when they look intentional.

**Palette (named colours):**

| Name | Hex | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Dusk blue | `#2B3A67` | street sky, shadows |
| Night blue | `#141B33` | ending sky |
| Lamp amber | `#F2B75B` | lamps, shop light |
| Warm window | `#FFD79A` | lit windows |
| Sea teal | `#2F7F86` | harbor water |
| Terracotta | `#B85C38` | roofs, pots |
| Cream paper | `#F1E6CF` | letters, sketchbook |
| Brass | `#C9A24E` | keys, clocks, charms |
| Ink | `#1B1F2A` | outlines, the ink pool, the pupil |
| Cat orange | `#D98C3F` | fur |
| Felt grey | `#8C8A85` | socks, dust |
| Moon silver | `#CFD6DC` | the moon |
| Eye gold | `#E3B341` | iris rings |

**Colour grade per place:** Street, blue dusk with warm windows. Shop, amber. Drawer, paper cream. Cat, warm orange and grey. Eye, deep blue with gold rings. Moon, silver. Ending, night blue with lit lamps.

**Style frames first.** Before the full art pass, three screenshots: the street, the drawer world, the cat's eye. The founder approves them (STOP B1).

**Forbidden:** real brands, logos, real places or people, franchise characters; words on signs (the shop sign is a painted cat's eye, the fish stall's board shows fish); anything that flashes more than 3 times a second.

**Audio (SHOULD):** procedural WebAudio only. Harbor sounds, a clock ticking, a purr, a soft space hum, the dive sound, the lock sound. Never required. Important sounds get captions.

## 8. Accessibility

- The whole chain can be played with the keyboard alone.
- Reduced motion replaces the dive with a cross-fade and stops decorative motion.
- No clue depends on colour alone or sound alone.
- UI text scales to 200%.
- "Describe surroundings" lists the landmarks in view and the usable things nearby. It never lists hidden or unfound things.
- Every sketch and every outline has alt text.

---

## 9. Save, restart and modes

- Autosave to `localStorage`. Restart clears it. If storage is blocked, the game warns once and continues in memory.
- `?perf=1` records frame times (every build). `?test=1` fixes the clock and pauses ambient motion. `?debug=1` (dev builds only) jumps to any place and shows state and FPS. The playtest build has no debug shortcut.
- The session log and the frame times are copied to the clipboard with "Copy log" and "Copy frame times". Downloads are blocked where the game is hosted.
- The title screen and the menu say: *Prototype · non-competitive · no prize*.

---

## 10. What step 1 is not

No walking, no flying, no model of the town, no past/present mechanic. No VR, multiplayer, accounts, prizes or leaderboards. No AI at runtime, no network calls, no public deployment. Those belong to step 2 or later, and step 2 needs its own go-ahead.
